Active master's thesis

Joan's active master's thesis is titled “AI-assisted Analysis and Explanation of Optimization Models.” It investigates how AI-assisted methods can help non-expert users understand existing mathematical optimization models and solver results. The starting point is an available MILP or MINLP model, not automatic model generation.

The research covers explanations of variables, constraint groups, solutions, objective-function trade-offs, binding constraints, infeasibility and irreducible infeasible subsystems (IIS), reduced costs, dual information where available, sensitivity to data changes, and differences between alternative solutions. Its central requirement is that natural-language explanations remain grounded in verifiable model and solver information rather than plausible but unsupported statements.

The research plan includes a literature review, a taxonomy of explanation tasks, a prototype for different user groups, grounding explanations in solver evidence, and evaluation on representative MILP or MINLP models for correctness, usefulness, and misleading-statement risk. These are active or planned thesis objectives, not confirmed milestones. Joan has not claimed that the literature review, taxonomy, prototype, evaluation, findings, or final results are complete.
